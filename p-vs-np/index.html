P versus NP, as a walk

Not a paper. A trail: every route walked until it opened or ended at a named failure point, with the failure points kept in the open. Nineteen sessions so far, each under a written charter, each verified by readers paid to kill it.

Calibration, stated first

There is no unconditional result in the notebook, and the notebook says so in its own front matter. The steward's estimate that P ≠ NP is about 98 percent; the estimate that this project produces the proof is about ε. What the project has produced is a map: a price list for what it costs to name a hard function, measured against the three classical barriers, with every closed road closed by evidence and every open one measured.

The thesis, hedged as it must be

Across constraint satisfaction, circuit complexity and meta-complexity, the presence of exploitable structure is what algorithms use and the absence of it is what hardness arguments turn on: a recurring shape and a partly shared toolkit, not one hardness in three costumes. The same dichotomy governs the tools, which is why the problem protects itself. Behind every stalled front stands one wall: a model admits structural lower bounds roughly to the extent that it cannot compute pseudorandom functions.

The one object

Every road on the map ends at the same place: the incompressible string. As a random string with no short description, as a hard truth table, as a non-output of the universal decompressor, as the formula no strong proof system is conjectured to prove. The recurring obstruction is not an oracle or a technique but a primitive: canonical selection, saying which hard object rather than that one exists. The field's newest results pay for it in two currencies, which string and which length, and the walk's last three swings priced both.

Near-maximum circuit lower bounds are known at S₂E, L₂E and Ren–Williams' class. They are open at MA_E with one bit of advice and at E^NP, which is the same as a deterministic algorithm with an NP oracle for the Range Avoidance problem, and which is impossible in the black-box world by an oracle from 1983. The Nineteenth Swing located, in two papers' own sentences, what the one advice bit pays for: in every such proof a prover hands over a small object encoding a canonical history; Li's object exists at every length unconditionally, and Ren–Williams' is guaranteed only when a walk has failed at another length, so the bit names the branch.

The method, in four lines

Hypotheses written before any source is opened, with the risky one pre-committed both ways. Primary texts read whole, never summaries. Every claim adversarially verified by readers paid to kill it. A failure point that keeps every spat form in the open, because the value of a failed attempt is the precision of its failure. The standing rule, earned over many swings: this project's product is cross-reading, and its failures are mechanisms. Not one mechanism proposed by the steward has survived; every finding that lasted was three sources read together saying what none says alone.

Where the notebook lives

The notebook, its charter, its per-swing hypotheses, verification reports, experiment scripts, a Lean formalisation and about a hundred primary sources are kept together in a repository. The repository stays private while its source shelf is tracked; the shelf is stripped before anything is made public. Until then this page is its public face, kept current by the steward.
